Reditus: the affiliate program you don't have to build yourself

The moment your own SaaS product has paying customers, "should I have an affiliate program" stops being hypothetical. Reditus is infrastructure for running one without building tracking, payouts, and a partner dashboard from scratch.

Affiliate management For SaaS founders From $59/mo
Verdict

Makes sense once you have any recurring revenue and want referrals to be trackable and payable without manual spreadsheet work. Not worth it pre-revenue — there's nothing yet for an affiliate to be paid a percentage of.

What it's actually for

Running an affiliate program means solving three unglamorous problems: attributing a signup to the right referrer, calculating what they're owed as customers renew or churn, and actually paying out on a schedule without doing it by hand. Reditus wraps all three into a dashboard plus a public marketplace listing, so people can find and apply to your program instead of you having to recruit every affiliate individually.

For a solo founder, the double use case is worth noting: you can run your own program through it, and — as covered in the SaaS programs list this site is built around — you can also join other founders' programs through the same marketplace as an affiliate yourself. It's infrastructure sitting on both sides of the same transaction.

Where it holds up

  • Handles attribution, recurring commission math, and payouts
  • Marketplace listing brings in affiliates you didn't have to recruit
  • Built specifically for SaaS recurring-revenue models, not one-off sales
  • Reasonable entry price relative to building this yourself

Where it doesn't

  • No value before you have paying customers to attribute referrals to
  • Another monthly line item on top of whatever you already pay for billing/analytics
  • Marketplace visibility helps, but doesn't replace actively recruiting affiliates who already reach your audience

Worth knowing: Reditus itself runs an affiliate program for Reditus, listed on this site alongside the tools it helps other founders sell. That's not a conflict — it's the same mechanism this whole site is built to document.
