A home drink station: sparkling water, Italian sodas, iced tea and cold brew

Working from home means every drink comes from your own kitchen. A small drink station makes that a pleasure: cold fizzy water on demand, a flavored soda when you want a treat, and a pitcher of iced tea or cold brew waiting in the fridge. It can cost less per glass than cans and café runs, and you decide exactly what goes in.

The short version

Start with one thing you will use every day. For most people that is either a sparkling water maker or a big pitcher for iced tea. Add a bottle or two of syrup and a glass with a lid, and you have a drink station. Everything else here is a nice extra.

The fizz

Sparkling water on demand, and the flavors to turn it into a soda.

1. A sparkling water maker

Fill the bottle with cold water, press, and you have sparkling water in a few seconds. No cans to carry home or recycle, and you choose how fizzy it is.

Hands-on: I have had a SodaStream Terra since August 2026 and I love it. The drinks come out fresh, crisp and bursting with nose-tickling bubbles. The best part is that you choose the flavor, or flavors, and whether it is sugar-free or extra sweet. 2. Flavored syrups

An Italian soda is simply flavored syrup and sparkling water over ice, with a splash of cream if you want a cream soda. A variety pack lets you find a favorite before committing to a big bottle of one flavor. Look for: a variety pack of four, and a sugar-free version if you are watching sugar.

The still drinks

Make a batch once, pour from the fridge all week.

3. An iced tea pitcher

A glass pitcher with a tight lid is the workhorse of a drink station. Use it for iced tea, lemonade, fruit water or a batch of cold brew. Look for: glass that will not hold flavors, an airtight lid so it can lie on a fridge shelf, and a size that fits your fridge door.

4. A cold brew maker

Cold brew is coffee steeped in cold water for 12 to 24 hours. It is smooth, less sharp than hot-brewed coffee, and keeps in the fridge for days, so one batch covers a working week. Also in the coffee station guide, with two pitcher-style alternatives. Look for: a reusable filter and a carafe that fits your fridge.

Storing and serving

The part that makes it look like a station and not a cluttered counter.

5. Flip-top glass bottles

For anything you make in a batch: homemade simple syrup, iced tea, lemonade, cold brew. The clamp stopper seals tight, and a row of them looks good on a shelf or in the fridge door. Look for: thick glass, spare seals, and a funnel and brush in the box.

6. Syrup dispensers

Pouring syrup from the original bottle gets sticky fast. Dispensers with a pump or a pour spout give you the same amount every time and keep the counter clean. Look for: glass, labels in the box, and a pump or spout that comes apart for washing.

Easy starter sets

  • Soda bar: sparkling water maker, a syrup variety pack, glass straws
  • Iced tea and cold brew: a pitcher, a cold brew maker, a glass cup with a lid
  • Homemade syrups: flip-top bottles for the batch, dispensers and a tray for the counter

Think twice

  • A sparkling water maker, until you know where you will swap the gas cylinders
  • More syrup flavors than you will finish. Start with one variety pack
  • Tall bottles and pitchers, until you have measured your fridge shelf
