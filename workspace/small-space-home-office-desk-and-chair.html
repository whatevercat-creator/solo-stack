Small-space home office: the desk, the chair and the comfort extras

Most home offices are a corner of another room. That makes two decisions matter more than any gadget: a desk that fits the space you have, and a seat you can stay in for a full working day. This guide covers both, plus the cushions and footrest that make an ordinary chair far more comfortable.

The short version

Measure first, then spend on the chair before the desk. If a new chair isn't in the budget, a seat cushion, a lumbar cushion and a footrest will improve the one you have for a fraction of the cost.

The desk

Measure the wall or corner first, then pick the shape that fits it. In a small room the desk decides where everything else goes.

1. A compact writing desk

The simplest answer for a spare corner or a bedroom wall: a plain top around 40 inches wide, sometimes with one drawer. Big enough for a laptop, a lamp and a notebook, and small enough to leave the room usable. Look for: a width that fits your wall with a few inches spare, a depth of at least 20 inches, and a crossbar that won't hit your knees.

2. An L-shaped corner desk

Corners are the most wasted space in a small room, and an L-shaped desk turns one into two work surfaces: one for the screen, one for paper or a second task. Many are reversible, so the long side can go on either wall. Look for: a reversible design, and measure both walls. The short side is often longer than it looks in photos.

Browse L-shaped desks on Amazon →

3. A folding or wall-mounted desk

For a room that has to be something else after work. A folding desk goes behind a door; a wall-mounted drop-leaf desk folds flat against the wall. Neither is a forever desk, but both beat the kitchen table. Look for: a stated weight limit that covers your laptop and your elbows, and solid wall fixings if it mounts.

The chair

The chair matters more than the desk. You notice a bad desk when you look at it and a bad chair all day.

4. An ergonomic mesh office chair

The safe default: a breathable mesh back, adjustable height and built-in lumbar support. You do not need the most expensive one, but adjustability is what separates a chair you can sit in for a day from one you can't. Look for: adjustable seat height and lumbar support, armrests that move or flip up, and a weight rating that covers you.

Browse ergonomic office chairs on Amazon →

5. A compact or armless desk chair

When the chair has to tuck fully under a small desk, armrests are what stop it. An armless or flip-up-arm chair takes up far less floor when you're not in it. Look for: flip-up arms or none, a seat that still adjusts for height, and wheels suited to your floor.

Browse compact desk chairs on Amazon →

6. A chair mat

Protects the floor, and on carpet it lets the chair roll instead of dig in. Unglamorous, and you will notice it within a day. Look for: the right type for your floor (carpet mats have grippers underneath; hard-floor mats are smooth), and a size that covers your full rolling range.

Browse chair mats on Amazon →

The comfort extras

Cheaper than a new chair, and often enough to rescue the one you have.

7. A seat cushion

A firm foam cushion takes pressure off on long days and can lift you an inch or two if the chair sits low. Many have a cut-out at the back to ease pressure on the tailbone. Look for: memory foam that holds its shape, a non-slip base and a removable, washable cover.

Browse seat cushions on Amazon →

8. A lumbar support cushion

Fills the gap between your lower back and the chair, which most ordinary chairs leave empty. Also in the desk setup guide, because it earns its place in both. Look for: a strap that holds it in place and a washable cover. Some sets pair it with a matching seat cushion.

Browse lumbar cushions on Amazon →

9. An under-desk footrest

If your feet don't rest flat on the floor, your legs and lower back make up the difference. A footrest fixes that, especially at a desk that is a little too high. Look for: an adjustable angle or height and a non-slip surface.

Before you buy

  • Measure the wall, the corner and the doorway the box has to come through
  • Check the desk height against your chair: most desks are 28 to 30 inches high
  • Read the return terms. Desks and chairs are bulky to send back

Think twice

  • A desk under 20 inches deep, unless it only ever holds a laptop
  • A chair with no height adjustment, however good it looks
  • Buying desk and chair together as a matched set. Choose each on its own merits
